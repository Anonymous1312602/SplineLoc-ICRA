Abstract

We present SplineLoc, a single-scan cross-view localization method that matches rover LiDAR against coarse lunar digital elevation models (DEMs) by representing both as continuous cubic B-spline height fields. Existing LiDAR global localization methods typically rely on reference-scan databases, while planetary approaches often require landmarks, temporal accumulation, or bounded initialization, making direct single-scan localization against coarse orbital DEMs challenging.

SplineLoc performs map-wide retrieval with an offset-invariant relative-height objective evaluated through masked Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) correlation, then refines retained candidates directly on measured LiDAR points against the continuous map spline. Experiments use four lunar sites built from real orbital topography, including NASA LROC NAC products, and evaluate DEM resolutions up to 10 m/pixel.

Dense local LiDAR vs. coarse global DEM. The sampling gap motivates a common continuous representation rather than direct dependence on matched scan density.

Contributions

The method is designed around one constraint: obtain a map-wide global pose fix from a single levelled rover LiDAR scan without a reference-scan database or bounded position initialization.

01

Common continuous height-field representation

A coarse orbital DEM and a single LiDAR scan are represented as cubic B-spline height fields, supporting continuous evaluation and analytic map gradients between the source samples.

02

Map-wide retrieval by masked FFT

An offset-invariant, mean-centred height objective decomposes into masked cross-correlations. This turns translation search at sampled headings into a small set of FFT correlations.

03

Coarse-map and native-product evaluation

Evaluation spans four lunar terrains, six DEM resolutions, a terrain-representation study, and a native orbital-product experiment using LROC NAC DTMs and a shape-from-shading DEM.

Method

0 · Map representation

The georeferenced DEM is fitted once per site with a cubic B-spline height field fM. The map knot spacing is hM = max(4 m, rD), preventing a coefficient grid finer than the source DEM when the reference map becomes coarse.

1 · Scan representation

The levelled LiDAR scan is fitted with a penalized cubic B-spline field fS. Retrieval samples both continuous fields onto a common 4 m lattice, with support restricted to cells near measured LiDAR points.

2 · Map-wide global retrieval

For each of 24 sampled headings, SplineLoc removes the mean scan-map height offset over valid overlap and evaluates the relative-height mismatch for all translations through masked cross-correlations. The lowest-cost K = 100 translation-heading hypotheses are retained.

3 · Local refinement and re-ranking

Each retained hypothesis is refined in continuous (cx, cy, ψ) using measured LiDAR points directly against fM. Gauss-Newton uses the analytic map gradient and the same mean-centred height-error form; refined candidates are re-ranked by the point-based cost.

Offset-invariant matchingThe overlap mean of the scan-map height difference is removed, so matching focuses on relative terrain-height variation rather than an unknown constant vertical offset.
Masked cross-correlationAfter expanding the mean-centred squared error, translation-dependent terms become discrete correlations, which are evaluated efficiently by FFT with zero-padding.
Measured-point refinementFinal optimization does not stay on the retrieval lattice. Continuous map evaluation permits sub-cell pose estimates without claiming to recover terrain detail absent from the DEM.

Lunar Evaluation

The evaluation uses a lunar simulation dataset initialized from real orbital topography. A fixed set of 100 query scans per site is reused across all methods and resolutions.

Apollo 11

300 × 320 m · 2 m/pixel source DTM

Weak, low-relief mare terrain. The map is comparable to the LiDAR footprint, testing whether distributed height variation remains sufficient without a prominent landmark.

Apollo 14

2000 × 2000 m · 2 m/pixel LROC NAC DTM

Crater relief over a large map, providing one of the primary map-wide retrieval settings.

Apollo 15

2000 × 2000 m · 2 m/pixel LROC NAC DTM

Directional relief near Hadley Rille; also a primary large-scale map-wide retrieval setting.

Haworth

400 × 400 m · 1 m/pixel SfS DEM

Extended crater-wall slopes with similar local surface normals; this is the most ambiguous terrain among the four sites.

Why the Continuous Representation Matters

Two additional studies separate the effect of terrain representation from the main resolution sweep and test whether the result persists when the simulation-derived reference map is replaced by its native orbital product.

Terrain representation under coarse DEMs

With the localization pipeline held fixed, the manuscript compares the B-spline field with bilinear and nearest-neighbor interpolation on Apollo 14 and Apollo 15 (200 pooled queries per condition).

Native orbital reference maps

The resolution sweep uses maps exported from the query-generating simulation terrain. The paper separately replaces these with native LROC NAC DTMs (Apollo) and the native SfS DEM (Haworth), plus area-mean downsampling to 5 and 10 m/pixel.
